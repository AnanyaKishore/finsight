User guide

FinSight brings three analyses of ten large US stocks together on one page: where the price has been, where a model thinks it may go over the next two weeks, and whether recent news about the company is positive or negative. It also suggests one way of splitting money across the ten stocks.

Finding a stock

  1. Click the search box at the top (or press /) and start typing a company name, its ticker symbol (for example AAPL) or a product (for example iPhone).
  2. Pick a suggestion with the mouse, or with the ↓ and ↑ keys and Enter. Esc closes the list.
  3. You can also click any of the ten stocks on the overview page.

FinSight covers Apple, Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Tesla, NVIDIA, Meta, JPMorgan Chase, Walmart and Visa.

The stock page

  • Overview: the latest closing price and the change from the day before. Green with an upward arrow means the price rose, red with a downward arrow means it fell. Prices are end-of-day, not live.
  • Price history: choose 1 week (1W) up to 1 year (1Y). Hover over the line to read the price on a day. “Show the prices as a table” lists every value.
  • 14-day forecast: the solid line is the real price; the dashed line is the model’s estimate for the next 14 trading days. The text under the chart says how accurate the model was in testing, compared with simply assuming the price will not change.
  • News sentiment: each bar is one week. Bars above the middle line mean news was mostly positive, bars below mean mostly negative. The small chart underneath shows how many articles there were. Click a bar to see the headlines behind it.

The optimised portfolio

The pie chart shows how a sum of money would be split across the stocks to get the best balance of return and risk (the highest Sharpe ratio) over the last 30 trading days. It is recalculated once a day.

  • Sharpe ratio: return earned per unit of risk. Higher is better.
  • Expected return: the yearly return the 30-day window suggests.
  • Volatility: how much the portfolio’s value swings, per year. Lower is calmer.

The reality check under the chart repeats the same method week by week over the past year and compares it with splitting money equally. Read it before trusting the pie.

Trying the sentiment model

On the overview page, paste one or more headlines (one per line) and press Analyse (or Ctrl+Enter). Each line gets a label (positive, negative or neutral), a score from −1 to +1, and the model’s confidence.

When something is not working

  • The dot at the top right shows the system status: green is normal, amber means one part is down, red means the portal cannot reach the server. Click it for details.
  • If a section says it could not load, press Try again. Other sections keep working.
  • If the forecasting model is down, you will see the last saved forecast, clearly marked as a saved copy.
  • A stock that is not one of the ten shows a “not covered” page.

Important

FinSight is a course project that demonstrates how machine-learning models are built and operated. Its forecasts and scores are estimates that are often wrong, and markets can move for reasons no model sees. Nothing here is a recommendation to buy or sell. Past performance does not guarantee future results.
